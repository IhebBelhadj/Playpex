This is the direction for this build. Adopt it. Do not blend it with the layout you would have made anyway,
and do not redraw because it is unfamiliar: unfamiliar is the point. Redraw a slot only when it conflicts
with a stated brand constraint or needs an asset that does not exist, and say why in your report.

The parts of this sheet were written separately and will disagree in small ways. Settle every disagreement in this order:
**house rules** (including type no smaller than 13px and body text of 16px or more) → **the direction** (the idea, the composition habits, where the
one accent goes) → **the type system and the palette** (which face does which job, the five size steps, which colour is the ground and where the
accent goes) → **the hero blueprint** → **the section blueprints**. Under each blueprint below, "Adjusted for this page" lists where it disagrees.
In practice: take geometry from blueprints (columns, proportions, what sits where) and take everything else from above them. Map every size a
blueprint names onto one of your five type steps instead of adding a sixth. Use one button shape, one corner radius and one pair of page margins on
the whole page, whatever individual blueprints say. If a blueprint names a kind of typeface (a light serif, a heavy grotesk), use the dealt face.

## 1. The idea
Every section is a chapter with its own flat colour, floor to ceiling. The page reads like a set of posters hung in a row, held together by one typeface and one grid. The colours are not decoration: they come from the subject (the brand's packaging, the artist's record sleeves, the product's own colour ways, the categories of the content) and each chapter's colour means that chapter. Decide the sequence like a designer orders a book: a strong opener, a quiet second, a surprise in the middle, a dark or very light closer.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
Then name the one live thing the visitor can do on the page (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
Sections are full-bleed fields, each at least 90svh tall, butted directly against each other with no gap and no divider. Inside every field the same 12-column grid and the same margins apply, and each field holds one thing: a huge heading (8–12vw) with a small paragraph, or one photograph with a caption, or one list. Text colour is chosen per field for strong contrast (ink or paper, never grey). Headings start on the same left edge in every chapter, so the eye feels the system as colours change. The navigation and the footer take the colours of the first and last chapter.

## 3. Type
Kept from the brand. One typeface in two weights. Headings very large and tight; body 18–20px. Because colour is loud, the type stays plain: no italics, no second face.

## 4. Colour
Kept from the brand. Four to six flat fields used at full strength, one per section, each with its assigned text colour. No tints, no gradients between them: the hard edge where two fields meet is the look.

## 5. Imagery
**This subject is software and there are no screenshots, so its pictures are its own interface.** Wherever this sheet says photograph, image or object, build a believable fragment of the product in HTML at real size (real labels, numbers that add up, one item hovered or selected) by the app-screen rules (references/app-screens.md: deal its style once with `node <skill>/scripts/draw.mjs --app --kind <kind> --keep palette,type --out .taste-skill/app`, and build it with assets/app.css and assets/charts.js), put the attribute `data-ui` on its root, and crop or scale it the way the blueprint treats a photograph: a full-bleed close crop, a tile, the fixed half of a split. Where a fragment makes no sense, use a flat colour field with type on it. Never a fake dashboard of grey bars, never a device frame. If the product works on something physical (ships, plants, buildings, a city), one real picture of that world can carry a section: find it with `node /home/iheb/projects/Playpex/.claude/skills/design-taste-frontend/scripts/assets.mjs "<that world>"`, download it with `--get`, credit it on the page, and never let it pass for the product or its customers.

Sparing. One photograph or object per chapter at most, either cut out and sitting directly on the colour, or a plain rectangle with generous colour around it. Photographs should be simple enough not to fight the field.

**Floor (the inspector checks it):** at least four real pictures on the page, usually six to twelve; SVG drawings do not count. One moving picture where an honest clip exists: `node <skill>/scripts/assets.mjs --video "<query>"`, used as a muted loop with a poster frame. Whatever this direction says about pictures, it is carried out with real ones.

## 6. Hero — Giant cropped shape
A plain ground with one flat shape at enormous scale. The shape is the simplest geometry found in the subject (a circle, a ring segment, a chamfered block, an arch), drawn in CSS or as a single inline SVG path and filled with the accent colour. It is about 120vh across and enters from the right, centred near 78% of the width and 55% of the height, so the top, right and bottom edges of the viewport crop it and its left boundary reaches column 7. If the subject has a true pair (two accounts, two modes), a second shape in a second flat colour overlaps the first by a quarter with `mix-blend-mode: multiply`. The headline sits on the bare ground on columns 1–6, vertically centred, flush left at 8% from the left edge: two lines at clamp(64px, 6.4vw, 100px), heavy weight, tracking -0.03em. Beneath it comes one supporting sentence at 19px, then the single action as a solid button cut to the same shape. A bold wordmark sits top-left at 32px; up to three text links sit top-right in the ground colour.

On a phone: The shape moves to the bottom: 130vw across, centred horizontally, its top at 56svh, cropped by the left, right and bottom edges. The headline sits above it at 11.5vw on two lines at a 16px margin, starting 15% down, with the sentence at 16px and the motif-shaped button beneath. Wordmark top-left; the links collapse to one word top-right.
Headline: Two lines of two or three words each, up to about 12 characters per line at 6.4vw.

## 7. Sections, in order
Each is a composition to rebuild with this project's content. Keep the geometry; change the subject. Re-order or drop a
section when the argument of the page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a card grid.

### 1. showcase — Output index, live sheet (index)
For products that make documents: invoices, reports, emails, statements. Columns 1–5 list the names of three to five outputs, one per line, at clamp(36px, 4vw, 64px), leading 1.1, each a `<button>`; the selected name is in full ink, the rest in the soft ink. There is no other heading. Columns 7–12 are a stage in a flat tone one step from the ground, 88svh tall, and on it lies the selected output as an object: a paper-white sheet 560px wide, built in HTML at true size, root marked data-ui, with a real sender, real line items, sums that add up and a date. The sheet starts 72px below the stage's top and runs past its bottom edge, which crops it. Selecting a name swaps the sheet with a 200ms crossfade; an email is narrower (440px), a receipt narrower still (300px), all left-aligned 64px into the stage.
On a phone: Names become one sideways-scrolling row at 22px with scroll-snap, the selected one in full ink. Beneath, the stage fills the width and is 70svh tall; the sheet keeps its true size, anchored top-left with a 16px inset and cropped by the stage's right and bottom edges. Tapping a name swaps it.

Adjusted for this page:
- It names colours (white): map them onto this palette's roles (ground `--bg`, panels `--surface` and the fields, text `--ink`/`--muted`, the accent `--accent` on the primary action only).

### 2. how-it-works — Accordion of tinted panels (strip)
A two-column introduction, then one horizontal accordion. Introduction: the heading, two to four words at clamp(36px, 4vw, 60px), in columns 1–5; one paragraph at 18px in columns 7–11. 64px below, a flex row across columns 1–12, 520px tall, of four to six panels with no gaps. The open stage is a panel taking about 58% of the width; the rest are vertical slivers sharing what remains. Order is carried by colour: the first stage is the darkest tone of one hue and each later stage is a lighter flat tint of it, so the row reads dark to light from left to right. A sliver shows only its stage name, rotated 90 degrees and reading upward, at 20px bold, anchored to its bottom-left. The open panel shows the name at 40px top-left, a 36ch paragraph at 18px bottom-left and, optionally, one photograph in its right half. Clicking a sliver opens it (flex-basis transition, 450ms).
On a phone: The accordion turns vertical: stages stack at full width as bars 64px tall in the same dark-to-light order, names set horizontally at 18px. The open stage expands to its content height with the paragraph at 17px. The first stage is open by default.

### 3. features — Fixed output, live controls (pinned)
A split the reader operates. Left, columns 1–6: a flat colour field that is position: sticky; top: 0; height: 100svh, edge to edge, holding one thing the product makes (a card, an email, a chart, a rendered page) centred at true size, about 420px wide, root marked data-ui. Right, columns 8–12: a reading column no wider than 44ch, with four or five capability blocks 50vh apart. Each block is a sentence at 28–32px, a paragraph at 17px, and directly beneath it one real control at interface size: a slider, a segmented choice, a toggle, a short text field. Operating a control changes the fixed output on the left at once, and changes persist, so by the last block the reader has configured their own. The section heading, two to four words at 40–44px, opens the right column, 72px from the top.
On a phone: The output sits in a sticky field at the top, 42svh tall, cropped to its most telling region, not shrunk. Blocks scroll beneath on the page ground: sentence at 22px, paragraph at 16px, control at full width with 44px targets. The field releases after the last block.

### 4. list — One item per poster line (statement)
A short list set as a poster. Four to seven items (services, headline acts, courses, opening days) stack one per line at clamp(64px, 10vw, 150px) in the display weight, all capitals or all lowercase, leading 0.92, flush left at a 20px margin on a flat field. Its one detail (a price, a date, a time range) sits at the right margin on the same baseline at 18–20px with tabular figures, so the page has two alignments only: giant words left, small facts right. Items too long for one line at this size are shortened, not shrunk. Between items there is only the tight leading. Hovering an item swaps its ink for the accent. There is no heading: the first item starts 80px below the section top and the last ends 80px above the bottom. The list reuses the hero title's exact type size, so it reads as another poster.
On a phone: Items at 15vw, one per line, flush left with 16px margins. Each detail moves beneath its item at 15px, left-aligned, with 6px above and 22px below. If any single word is wider than the screen at 15vw, drop the whole list to 12vw rather than breaking a word.

Adjusted for this page:
- It names a kind of typeface: use the brand faces.

### 5. cta — First step, already open (split)
The page ends with the product's first screen already open. A full-bleed field in the accent colour, 90svh tall. Right, in columns 7–11, vertically centred: the product's own create dialog at true size, 440px wide, root marked data-ui, with its real title ('Name your workspace'), one text field holding an editable, believable example, one two-option segmented choice and its primary button. Inside the fragment the product's conventions apply, including the shadow its dialog normally casts. The field accepts typing, the choice switches, and the primary button is the page's only action: it carries the typed value into sign-up. Left, in columns 1–5, anchored to the bottom with 40px margins: the closing line, two to four words at clamp(56px, 7vw, 112px), leading 0.92, on two lines, and under it one sentence at 17px saying what happens after the button. The upper-left stays empty.
On a phone: The closing line leads at 13vw on two lines, top-left, with its sentence beneath at 16px. The dialog follows at full width inside 16px margins, at true size, its primary button 52px tall and full width. The field is at least 84svh; the dialog sits in its lower half.

Adjusted for this page:
- It mentions a shadow: only on something open above the page (a menu, a dialog), never on a resting element.

### 6. footer — Contact as the headline (statement)
A full-bleed field in one saturated palette colour, at least 90svh tall. Top row, 40px from the edge: two or three short notes at 15px in columns 1–7 (address, hours, a one-line description) and a back-to-top text link at the right margin. Then a tall empty gap. In the lower half, the way to get in touch is the display line: the telephone number or the email address itself, set at 8–9vw, flush left, hard against the margin, as one link. An email address may break once, before the @, giving two lines. Size it with `clamp()` so the longest line fills at least 80% of the width. Under it, one row of small text at 14px spread across three positions: links at the left, credits in the centre, city and year at the right. The wordmark appears only small, in the top row.
On a phone: Notes stack at the top at 15px. The contact line sits in the lower half at 12vw; a telephone number stays on one line, an email breaks before the @ and is sized so each half fits the width. The meta row becomes three stacked lines at 13px.

## 8. Motion — Shutter image
The one hero or showcase image opens from an edge with a clip-path over about 1.1 seconds while the picture inside settles from 1.12 scale to rest. Use it once, on the image that matters most; later images simply are there. Recipe: 'Image shutter' in references/motion.md.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): an entrance for the parts of every section, feedback on every control, and one moving picture. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

## 10. Where the richness comes from
Colour at scale. A whole screen of one saturated colour is rich on its own, so a chapter needs few elements; it still needs its content at full size, because a coloured screen with one small line on it reads as empty.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
Check contrast on every field with the palette's assigned ink. Do not put cards on the fields. Do not use more than six colours. Two neighbouring fields should differ clearly in lightness as well as hue.

## Always
Headlines two lines or fewer at every width. No eyebrow labels, no monospace, no 01/02/03, no thin rules, no gradients, no shadows at rest,
no cream-and-terracotta, no violet. The frame: one h1 on the first screen that says what this is, equal outer margins, no
section huddled on one side with the other half empty, no blank stretch over half a screen, a heading on every section.
`inspect.mjs` measures all of it.
